Hardware-Efficient Multipliers With FPGA-Based
Approximation for Error-Resilient Applications
Yi Guo
 , Member, IEEE, Qilin Zhou, Xiu Chen, Student Member, IEEE, and Heming Sun
 , Member, IEEE
Abstract— Approximate multipliers enable hardware savings
for error-resilient computation-intensive applications. Most exist-
ing approximate multipliers have been on ASIC-based circuits.
They might not achieve comparable performance gains when
used for FPGA-based accelerators. In this paper, we propose
hardware-efficient FPGA-based accurate and approximate 4 ×
4 multipliers with novel methodologies of look-up table (LUT)
sharing and carry switching. The LUT resources can be fully uti-
lized by sharing two LUTs with the same inputs. To compensate
for the accuracy loss, the truncated carry is partially reserved by
switching it to the adjacent calculation. For higher-order multi-
pliers, three approximate adders are proposed to sum the result of
the multipliers with arbitrary size. 140 types of 8 × 8 multipliers
are constructed by combining the proposed 4 × 4 multipliers
and adders, providing various multiplication choices for different
demands. The proposed approximate 8 × 8 multiplier can achieve
up to 38.75%, 17.29%, and 28.17% improvements in power,
latency, and area over the Xilinx exact multiplier, respectively.
Moreover, the proposed accurate and approximate 8 × 8 multi-
pliers with different adders are extended to 16 × 16 multipliers.
As evidenced by the performance of the 16 × 16 multipliers,
our methodology demonstrates the capability to design higher-
order multipliers flexibly. Compared with previous works under
a similar accuracy loss, the proposed multiplier achieves more
hardware savings. Furthermore, the approximate multipliers are
assessed on the application of image processing to validate the
practical applicability. We create a library of the proposed mul-
tipliers which is open-source at https://github.com/YnuGuoLab
/Approx_Mul_FPGA/ and assist in further reproducing and
development.
Index Terms— Approximate computing, FPGA, multiplier.

Most state-of-the-art works are introduced concentrating on
ASIC-based circuits. Zhang et al. [1] propose an inexact four-

An approximate unsigned integer multiplier is presented

method with the classical log-based multiplier. Design of
approximate adders and multipliers based on machine learning

bits. Ansari et al. [8] use approximate adders to construct two
optimized logarithmic multipliers. Mrazek et al. [9] present a
library of 8-bit approximate adders and multipliers.

FPGA-based system design.

including signed and unsigned multipliers, which exploit

A. Motivational Case Study
High latency caused by multiple look-up table (LUT) layers
and carry chains in the accurate multiplier. Approximate mul-
tipliers are typically introduced based on accurate multipliers.
However, the traditional FPGA-based accurate multiplier usu-
ally produces high latency, because of complicated LUT layers
and long carry propagation. For instance, the critical path of
an accurate multiplier [14] is occupied by three LUTs and
three carry chains with strong correlations, undermining the
efficiency of approximate computing on the multiplier. In order
to present higher-performance approximate multipliers, the
first need is to propose an optimized accurate multiplier.
Large accuracy loss incurred by the elimination of
LUTs and carry chains in the approximate multiplier. The
works [11], [13], [14], [15], [16] present the low-complexity
approximation methods by deleting LUTs or carry chains and
indeed achieve hardware savings. However, due to the lack
of carry propagation, most of these multipliers are facing
one common problem of large accuracy loss. Hence, the
present challenge lies in devising approximate multipliers
with enhanced accuracy by computing the carry as much as
possible.
The polarized performance of the constructed multipliers
caused by accuracy-hardware imbalance from adders. Build-
ing a large-scale multiplier from sub-multipliers and an adder
is a common method. However, this recursive construction
might result in additional hardware overhead for the approx-
imate multipliers [11], [13], [15] compared with traditional
multipliers. Therefore, it is necessary to propose adders with
various levels of precision, ranging from high to low smoothly
trading accuracy for hardware.
Narrow performance scope of the library due to the scarce
configurations in building high-order multipliers. In [15],
a library of approximate 8 × 8 multipliers is constructed
by combining four inexact 4 × 4 multipliers and one adder.
However, only 35 types of configurations with similar accuracy
are provided, which fail to meet the diverse demands of
different error-tolerant applications. Thus, there is a necessity
to provide a library with a greater variety of configurations to
fulfill various performance requirements.
B. Our Novel Contributions
To address the aforementioned issues, we propose an
FPGA-based multiplier design with novel approximation
methodologies. The main contributions are as follows:
1) An accurate 4 × 4 multiplier is proposed with a LUT-
oriented optimization, which has a structure of fewer LUT
layers. The critical path is occupied by only two LUTs and one
carry chain, leading to lower latency than previous accurate
multipliers.
2) Based on the accurate 4 × 4 multiplier, we propose the
approximate multipliers with methodologies of LUT sharing
and carry switching. In the proposed approximate multiplier,
two LUTs with the same inputs are shared and combined into
one LUT to save the hardware resource. Additionally, the carry
in the multiplier is switched into the adjacent computation
instead of the complete truncation. Because of the proposed
approximation methodologies, significant hardware savings
can be achieved under an acceptable accuracy loss.
3) Three types of approximate adders are proposed with a
steady and slow decline in accuracy, yet progressive hardware
improvements. These three adders can be used to construct
large-scale multipliers with arbitrary sizes. In the adder design,
a LUT-specific carry prediction scheme is proposed and inte-
grated. This approach maximizes resource utilization when
summing the results from sub-multipliers.
4) A library of the proposed multipliers is created by com-
bining small-size multipliers and adders, generating diverse
different configurations. Due to the abundant configurations,
the performance scope of this library is wide enough to meet
various requirements. This library is open-source and available
online at https://github.com/YnuGuoLab/Approx_Mul_FPGA/
to encourage and help further research in this direction.
We provide an overview in Fig. 2, demonstrating how our
proposed approach achieves the above contributions. Section II
presents the preliminaries required for understanding the
proposed method. Based on the structure of primary logic
resources presented in Section II, Section III introduces the
proposed accurate 4 × 4 multiplier with fewer LUT layers,
and also further explores the high-efficiency approximate
4 × 4 multipliers with acceptable accuracy loss. In Section IV
higher-order multipliers are constructed by the proposed 4 ×
4 multipliers and four types of adders. The experiment results
and discussions of the multipliers are shown in Section V
and VI. Section VII concludes this paper.

computational blocks on modern FPGAs which are utilized for
implementing any types of sequential or combinatorial circuits.
One CLB is composed of two slices, with each slice containing
four 6-input LUTs and one associated carry chain. As shown in
Fig. 3(a), a 6-input LUT can be utilized as one of the following
two implementations. The first one is referred to as LUT6
which implements a 6-input combinational function with a
single output O. Another one is named LUT6_2 which has
two 5-input combinational functions with two outputs O5 and
O6. Any type of LUT can be used for different requirements.
For instance, when there is a demand to implement a logic
function with 6 inputs, it can be achieved by employing a
stand-alone LUT6. In cases where two logic functions share
the same 5 inputs, their functions can be simultaneously
accomplished using a single LUT6_2. The function of a
LUT6 or a LUT6_2 can be defined by an INIT attribute
which consists of 16 hexadecimal values (i.e. 64 binary values
for 64 input combinations). The INIT value is determined
by creating a binary logic table of all input combinations.
It describes that the logic value ‘1’ occurs on the outputs
among all 64 combinations. For example, an INIT value of
‘0000000000000004’ (hex) for LUT6 means that the output
O is ‘1’ for the input combination ‘000010’.
In FPGAs, the carry chain usually connects to four 6-input
LUTs. As shown in Fig. 3(b), four LUTs produce the
carry-generate and carry-propagate signals. That is, outputs
O5 and O6 of LUTs are as carry-generate and carry-propagate
signals for the carry chain, respectively. AX, BX, CX, and DX
are the bypass signals connecting another slice. Their states
are inactive by default. Hence, the carry-generate signals of
the carry chain are driven by the output O5 of the LUT6_2.
The carry chain performs the function of a 4-bit carry look-
ahead adder. The output of each column is produced with XOR
operation on the carry-propagate signal and the carry from the
previous column.

C. Carry Switched Approximate 4 × 4 Multiplier (CSAM)
With No Carry Chain
The carry chain is efficient to perform the addition, while it
consumes dominant hardware resources in the multiplier [15].
Designing approximate multipliers with no carry chain is
a hardware-efficient method yet incurs an increase in error.
To lower the accuracy loss caused by no carry chain, we pro-
pose the methodology of carry switching to partially reserve
the carry signal instead of complete carry truncation.
1) Carry Switched Approximate Multiplier 1 (CSAM1):
Fig. 6 shows the structure of CSAM1 that is implemented
by 10 LUTs. The methodology of carry switching is applied
on the calculations of P3 and P4 as introduced as follows.
CSAM1 is designed based on LSAM2, in which calculat-
ing higher-order results ( P7 ∼ P4) requires the carry from
lower-order columns (columns 3 ∼0). This carry is calculated
as:
carry term 1 = L13L21 + L13L12 A0 B2 + L21L12 A0 B2 (4)

The exact calculations for P3 and P4 are expressed as :
P3−exact = L13 ⊕ L21 ⊕ (L12 A0 B2) (5)
P4−exact = L14 ⊕ L22 ⊕ carry term 1 (6)
It can be seen that the number of elements for calculating
P4−exact exceeds the maximum number of inputs in LUT6. For
calculating P4 by one LUT6, carry term1 is truncated. In this
way, the value of P4 is wrong when carr y term 1 equals to ‘1’.
This involves two conditions: condition 1 , two of L13, L21,
and L12 A0 B2 are ‘1’; condition 2, all the value of L13, L21,
and L12 A0 B2 are ‘1’. The combination of the errors in both
conditions results in an excessive accuracy loss. Fortunately,
elements in carry term1 are the same as those used to compute
P3−exact . To reduce the error caused by the truncation of
carry term1 from the logic expression of P4, carry term1 is
switched to the logic expression of P3 instead of complete
truncation directly.
There are two methods to dispose switched carry term1.
Method 1 is that ‘ P3 + carry term1’ (i.e. OR operation on P3
and carry term1), while method 2 is ‘ P3 ⊕ carry term1’ (i.e.
XOR operation on P3 and carry term1). The errors caused
by condition 1 and condition 2 can be partially compensated
by method 1 and method 2 , respectively. Obviously, the
occurrence probability of condition 1 is higher than that of
condition 2 . Hence, carr y term 1 is switched from P4 to P3
by method 1 of ‘ P3 + carry term1’. P3 and P4 in CSAM1 are
calculated as:

P4−C S AM1 = L14 ⊕ L22 (8)
